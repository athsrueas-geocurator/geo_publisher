## Reuse and enrich existing coverage

- **Reading First Impact Study: Final Report** (Gamse et al., 2008, NCEE 2009-4038) is already `src-015`. [Official report](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/evaluation-report/reading-first-impact-study-final-report), [design/access](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/evaluations/reading-first-impact-study). Regression-discontinuity evidence found instructional and first-grade decoding improvements but no statistically significant comprehension effect in grades 1–3. Keep outcomes separate; nonsignificance is not proof of zero benefit. A matched intervention cost source is still needed. The evaluation contract award is not program delivery cost. IES identifies restricted-use replication data.
- **Head Start Impact Study** is already `src-071`; add the distinct third-grade follow-up. [Official follow-up](https://acf.gov/sites/default/files/documents/opre/head_start_report_0.pdf), [summary](https://acf.gov/sites/default/files/documents/opre/head_start_executive_summary.pdf). Early gains followed by few measured effects through third grade provide essential context. Preserve cohort, access/treatment contrast, alternative care and follow-up horizon. This does not prove zero lifetime benefit. Incremental costs and data access still need checking.
- **Tutoring meta-analysis**: Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan, [2020 working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476), [2024 publication](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00028312231208687). The source file repeats DOI `10.3386/w27476` at `src-068` and `src-093`; resolve both to one publication identity and link the journal version explicitly. Use the synthesis for heterogeneity/context, not to assign every tutoring program the same cost/effect. Do not treat the synthesis and included trials as independent confirmations.

## Proposed first publication package

Begin with Saga's paired costs/outcomes, STAR's experimental/economic papers, Perry's economic analysis and Reading First's mixed outcomes. Follow with finance reforms, Abecedarian/CARE and Head Start follow-up. These cover several intervention models; they do not supply complete current costs and outcomes for all 76 initiatives.

Represent study/publication → implementation/site/cohort → cost observation → outcome estimate → separately modeled economic estimate, each with source version and table/page provenance. Each cost needs currency, price year, period, denominator, incremental/total scope, perspective and included resources. Each outcome needs unit, instrument, population, time horizon, design, ITT/TOT distinction and uncertainty. Economic models need discount rate, horizon, benefits and sensitivity assumptions. Unknown fields remain null; do not rank unlike constructs as one efficiency score.

Cross-space exact-title probes for Perry and Saga returned no matches. These limited searches do not establish absence: complete DOI/version/alias and canonical-identity matching before creating anything. Preserve the source aliases above and reuse existing entities across spaces.

Publish factual metadata, original summaries and sourced extracted observations first; link to reports. Full-document/raw-data uploads require separate redistribution checks. Public reading access does not imply permission to republish a paper or restricted microdata. No Geo publication occurred during this research.

Youth ChalleNGe cost-benefit follow-up, 2026-09-12: DOL CLEAR’s profile of Perez-Arce et al. (2012) identifies a same-ten-site cost-benefit model. It reports $11,633 2010 operating cost per admittee, $15,436 total societal cost, $40,985 modeled societal benefits, $25,549 modeled net benefit, and 166% ROI. This is a high-value follow-up because it can connect costs and outcomes, but it remains a projected lifetime model rather than a second causal study. Do not merge it with the three-year impact Article or rank it against other programs until structure/assumptions are extracted.

CUNY ASAP candidate, 2026-09-12: MDRC’s 2020 eight-year cost paper follows the CUNY randomized trial, not the separate Ohio replication. It reports $13,838 net cost per program-group member and $9,162 additional cost per degree over eight years. MDRC’s project page reports 18-percentage-point three-year and 10-percentage-point six-year graduation effects for the original CUNY evaluation. Preserve the CUNY trial, its 2020 cost paper, the Ohio demonstration, and 2026 CUNY 14-year report as distinct study/version lineages; observed graduation outcomes, financial-aid accounting, and longer-run earnings are not interchangeable measures.
