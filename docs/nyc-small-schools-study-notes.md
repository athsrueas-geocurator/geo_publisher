# New York City Small Schools of Choice: longitudinal outcomes and cost evidence

Primary outcome source: MDRC, *Enduring Success: Effects of New York City’s Small Schools of Choice on Postsecondary Degree Attainment and Employment* (January 2026), https://www.mdrc.org/work/publications/enduring-success and https://www.mdrc.org/sites/default/files/SSC_Enduring_Success.pdf.

Primary cost source: Howard Bloom and Rebecca Unterman, *The Relative Costs of New York City’s New Small Public High Schools of Choice* (MDRC, 2014), https://nocache.mdrc.org/work/publications/relative-costs-new-york-citys-new-small-public-high-schools-choice/file-full.

## Verified extraction scope — 2026-09-12

The outcome report follows students applying to Small Schools of Choice (SSCs) in the 2005–2006 through 2008–2009 admissions years. It uses lottery outcomes within the New York City high-school assignment process as an instrument for SSC enrollment, comparing students who won and enrolled with students who lost and did not enroll. This is a single SSC evaluation lineage, not a collection of independent studies for each outcome or report.

Figure 1 reports results for **16,496** rising ninth-graders. SSC enrollment increased four-year high-school graduation by **8.2 percentage points** (76.3% versus 68.1%); enrollment in postsecondary education within six months of expected high-school graduation by **9.5 points** (52.9% versus 43.4%); four-year postsecondary degree attainment within four years by **2.4 points** (6.8% versus 4.4%); and four-year degree attainment within six years by **2.5 points** (14.7% versus 12.2%). The report states that certificate/two-year-degree attainment and employment/earnings did not show discernible effects. It cautions that earnings records are unavailable for every student.

The 2014 cost paper evaluates the same broader SSC model but uses earlier cohorts for its cost analysis. Its experimental cost comparison reports five-year direct-service expenditures of **$57,246** for target SSC enrollees and **$59,818** for their control counterparts, paired with five-year graduation rates of 74% and 65%, respectively; it reports $77,780 versus $92,454 cost per graduate. The paper explicitly says facilities, external partner resources, and startup investments were not fully accounted for, and it cannot estimate the counterfactual cost of never undertaking the small-schools initiative. The publication year does not establish a common dollar price year for these expenditure estimates. Any Geo records must retain that price-year limitation and must not convert them into a benefit-cost ratio, universal savings, or a claim about all small-school models.
