# Male Student Success Initiative (MSSI), MDRC 2023

Primary source: Lashawn Richburg-Hayes et al., *Pathway to College Achievement: A Mixed-Methods Evaluation of the Male Student Success Initiative for Men of Color* (MDRC, September 2023), https://www.mdrc.org/work/publications/pathway-college-achievement/file-full.

The evaluation randomly assigned 514 eligible Community College of Baltimore County students who identified as male students of color: 304 to an offer of the two-semester MSSI program and 210 to usual CCBC services. MSSI combined a culturally contextualized Academic Development 101 course, success mentoring, support-service referrals, leadership and career activities, and community-building. It was evaluated from 2019 through spring 2022; the control group retained services available to all CCBC students, so this is not a no-service comparison.

Publish factual Table ES.1/4.1 findings separately: first-semester ACDV-course enrollment +10.3 percentage points (66.4% versus 56.1%, p=.012, SE 4.1); first-semester ACDV-course passing +15.2 points (43.4% versus 28.1%, p<.001, SE 4.2); and first-year credits earned +0.3 in each semester, neither statistically significant (p=.446 and p=.496). Publish the later, cohorts-1-to-3 fourth-semester finding separately: earning an A, B, or C in all courses +7.3 points (12.5% versus 5.2%, p=.048, SE 3.6; N=256). The closely related +6.3-point version excluding ACDV (p=.092) should remain a separate sensitivity/context record if included; it does not establish an independent effect.

The program’s overall cost is **$885 per intended program participant**, in real 2021 dollars, across spring 2019 through fall 2021. This is an intent-to-treat service cost: the denominator includes recruited students and students in their second eligible term, whether or not they enrolled. It derives from time logs, job-posting wage/salary information, budget data, and a 12-week-per-term mentor assumption; cost logs varied in quality. The report says early persistence/completion impacts were absent and **cost effectiveness could not be determined**, so do not publish a benefit-cost ratio, cost per outcome, saving, or ranking.

The report documents inconsistent implementation and a major COVID-19 disruption. About 40% of the sample experienced the program in person and 60% during the pandemic; this context and the changed treatment contrast are material limits. The source identifies CCBC in Baltimore County, Maryland. Use canonical Maryland state geography only unless a complete all-space identity check supports the college identity; do not infer campus coordinates or a public outreach stop.
