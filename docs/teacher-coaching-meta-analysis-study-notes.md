# Teacher coaching meta-analysis evidence notes

Primary paper retrieved 2026-09-12 from the authors' University of Maryland-hosted PDF: Kraft, Blazar, and Hogan (2018), *The Effect of Teacher Coaching on Instruction and Achievement: A Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence*, DOI `10.3102/0034654318759268`.

The review includes 60 causal studies: 56 randomized trials and four quasi-experiments. It reports 186 instructional-practice effect sizes from 43 studies and 113 achievement effect sizes from 31 studies. It is a synthesis, not one new teacher-coaching intervention or one randomized trial.

Table 2 reports pooled standardized effects with robust variance-estimated standard errors. For all studies, coaching has an instructional-practice estimate of 0.488 SD (SE 0.056; 186 effect sizes; 43 studies) and an achievement estimate of 0.178 SD (SE 0.037; 113 effect sizes; 31 studies). The achievement results include reading 0.163 SD (SE 0.032; 87 effects; 26 studies), math 0.044 SD (SE 0.042; 20 effects; 5 studies), and science 0.352 SD (SE 0.242; 6 effects; 3 studies).

The content-specific and general-practice rows are overlapping subgroup estimates. Content-specific coaching has 0.512 SD for instruction (SE 0.061; 119 effects; 27 studies) and 0.197 SD for achievement (SE 0.041; 102 effects; 26 studies); general-practice coaching has 0.466 SD for instruction (SE 0.109; 67 effects; 16 studies) and 0.068 SD for achievement (SE 0.056; 11 effects; 5 studies). The paper marks all but math, science, and general-practice achievement as statistically significant at p < .001; it does not print an exact p value for the unstarred cells.

For Geo, each row needs its Table 2 locator, effect unit, SE, effect-size count, and study count. A dashboard can compare the labeled pooled estimates but must not aggregate or vote-count their overlapping studies. A nonfactual parent should ask whether districts should invest in sustained teacher coaching and use the overall instruction/achievement estimates as support, with the general-practice achievement and scale-up qualification as challenge/context. Do not claim a causal effect for individual coaching features from the paper's descriptive meta-regressions.
