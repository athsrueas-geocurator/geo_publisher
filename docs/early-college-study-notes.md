# Early College High Schools: lottery follow-up and cost-benefit evidence

Primary outcome source: Mengli Song and Kristina Zeiser et al., *Early College, Continued Success: Longer-Term Impact of Early College High Schools* (AIR, 2019; ERIC ED602451), https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED602451.pdf.

Official study record: Institute of Education Sciences, [AIR Early College Follow-Up Efficacy Study](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/air-early-college-follow-efficacy-study).

## Verified extraction scope — 2026-09-12

The study follows **2,458** lottery applicants at ten Early College High Schools in five states, from admissions cohorts in 2005–06, 2006–07, and 2007–08: 1,044 offered enrollment and 1,414 not offered. It estimates intent-to-treat effects of an offer, with separate complier estimates. All factual core Claims must use the ITT finding and must not represent the treatment-on-treated estimate as an independent study.

By Year 10 (six years after expected high-school graduation), the ITT report gives:

- Any college enrollment: **84.2% versus 77.0%, +7.2 percentage points**, p < .001 (Exhibit F-1, PDF p. 76).
- Any postsecondary degree: **45.4% versus 33.5%, +11.9 points**, p < .001 (Exhibit F-3, PDF p. 79).
- Associate degree or certificate: **29.3% versus 11.1%, +18.2 points**, p < .001 (Exhibit F-3, PDF p. 79).
- Bachelor’s degree: **30.1% versus 24.9%, +5.2 points**, p = .037 (Exhibit F-3, PDF p. 79).
- Four-year-college enrollment: **57.6% versus 56.7%, +0.8 points**, p = .744 (Exhibit F-1, PDF p. 76). This is an appropriate factual null/context record, not proof of no effect on any postsecondary outcome.

IES identifies a separate AIR cost-benefit source: Atchison, Zeiser, Mohammed, Levin, and Knight (2019), *The Costs and Benefits of Early College High Schools*. The IES study record reports approximately **$3,800 more per student over four years** than traditional high school and approximately **$58,000 average lifetime benefit per student** attributed to increased educational attainment. These are modelled lifetime values, not observed earnings and not another causal study.

## Cost-paper inspection — 2026-09-12

The primary paper (ERIC ED606242) verifies that all monetary figures are CPI-adjusted **2017 USD**. Its preferred model reports $57,682 total lifetime benefits per student ($33,709 private plus $23,973 public), a $955 midpoint annual incremental cost and $3,819 over four high-school years, $53,863 net present value, and a 15.1 benefit-to-cost ratio. The conservative scenario reports $34,834 benefits, $7,615 four-year cost, $27,219 NPV, and a 4.6 ratio (pp. 11–15; Figure 4).

These are source-reported model outputs. Benefits are constructed by multiplying estimated postsecondary-attainment differences by return estimates from prior studies; the cost comparison is available for **six** of the ten Early Colleges in the impact study. It includes school spending, college instructional costs, and incremental dual-enrollment administration, but does not fully capture centralized nonadministrative costs such as transportation (pp. 11–15, 31–33). The source’s simulations model uncertainty, but these values must never be presented as observed earnings, a participant payment, a universal Early College result, or a second independent causal study.
